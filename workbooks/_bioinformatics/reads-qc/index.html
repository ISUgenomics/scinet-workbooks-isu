This module focuses on **sequencing read quality control** for **short-read** and **long-read** data, as well as methods for summarizing QC results across samples. Before working through this module, review [Reads, contigs, scaffolds, and nucleotide sequences](/bioinformatics/resources/nomenclature) for the core terminology needed to interpret read-level QC concepts and results.
## Overview Before you align reads, quantify transcripts, or compare samples, you need to know whether the sequencing data are structurally sound, biologically plausible, and free of technical problems that will distort later results. The goal is not to make every warning disappear but to determine whether the reads are trustworthy enough for the intended downstream task, and if not, what minimal preprocessing is needed to correct the problem. This introduction page shows how classic sample-level read quality assessment, trimming, filtering, and QC summarization fit into bioinformatics workflows, and where upstream platform-native preprocessing steps may belong before that QC begins. {% include overviews %} ## Where QC fits in the workflow Read-level quality control is an early checkpoint between platform-native sequencing outputs and meaningful downstream analysis. In some workflows, basecalling, demultiplexing, or structural cleanup must come first so that QC is performed on the per-sample reads that will actually be analyzed.
#### Platform-native preprocessing *(if needed)* Start here when the workflow still depends on native sequencing outputs rather than read-level files, for example ONT raw signal, PacBio upstream processing, demultiplexing, or read generation. #### Raw sequencing data In most projects, bioinformatics analysis starts here after you receive per-sample read files from the sequencing facility or provider. These are typically exported reads such as `FASTQ` or read-space `BAM`, ready for sample-level QC. #### Read quality control (QC) Inspect quality, sequence composition, adapter contamination, duplication, and other technical signals before changing the data. #### Trimming or filtering *(if needed)* Apply preprocessing only when the QC results point to a specific problem that should be corrected before downstream analysis. #### Read QC after trimming Check the processed reads again to confirm that preprocessing improved the data without removing more sequence than necessary. #### Downstream analysis Continue with the analysis step that matches your data and study design, such as alignment, quantification, assembly, or variant discovery.

control matters before analysis Quality control matters before bioinformatics analysis because each QC signal can point to a different technical problem, and may require a different response before downstream steps. **Common QC patterns in short-read data** | QC signal | What it may affect | Typical next action | |-----------|--------------------|---------------------| | Low-quality tails | it may affect | Typical next action | |-----------|--------------------|---------------------| | Broad read-length variation | assembly continuity, transcript model completeness, yield expectations | inspect length distribution and decide whether minimum-length filtering is useful | | Lower per-read quality | alignment accuracy, consensus quality, downstream confidence | inspect per-read quality profiles and consider quality-based filtering only if needed | | Adapter or barcode remnants | read structure, demultiplexing accuracy, downstream cleanup | confirm whether cleanup belongs in an upstream platform-native workflow such as `Lima` or `Dorado`, or in exported-read cleanup | | Barcoded read pools needing sample separation | sample assignment, downstream per-sample QC, barcode-aware interpretation | decide whether barcode demultiplexing belongs in the upstream platform workflow before downstream QC interpretation | | Platform-specific terminal artifacts | end-sequence interpretation, retained read usability | inspect the pattern first, then choose targeted trimming only when it improves downstream use | | Excess very short reads | reduced mappability, lower assembly value, noisier summaries | filter by length when short reads add little value to the planned analysis |
**A failed or warning-heavy QC module does not always mean the sample is unusable.** Interpret the pattern first, then decide whether trimming, filtering, or simply documenting the limitation is the most appropriate response.
## Reads preprocessing Quality assessment, trimming, filtering, and post-trimming validation are related steps, but they do not serve the same purpose. | Step | What it does | Typical short-read use | Typical long-read use | |------|--------------|------------------------|-----------------------| | quality assessment | inspects the reads without changing them | identify low-quality tails, adapter contamination, GC bias, duplication, or pairing issues | inspect read-length distribution, per-read quality, adapter/barcode remnants, or platform-specific artifacts | | trimming | removes sequence from the reads | remove adapters, low-quality ends, poly-G tails, or assay-specific artifacts | remove adapters or barcodes, or trim targeted terminal artifacts when they interfere with downstream analysis | | filtering | removes whole reads that do not meet criteria | discard reads that become too short or fail quality thresholds after preprocessing | remove reads below chosen length or quality thresholds before assembly, mapping, or summarization | | post-trimming validation | checks whether preprocessing improved the data | confirm that adapter content and poor-quality regions were reduced without over-trimming | confirm that filtering or trimming improved the read set for the planned downstream task | ## Find your next step

{% include accordion title="decide whether trimming is needed at all" controls="rqc6" expanded=false class="outline" icon=false %}
Trimming is needed when the QC results show a technical problem that is likely to interfere with downstream analysis and can be corrected without removing more sequence than necessary. For **short-read data**, common reasons to trim include adapter contamination from short inserts, low-quality 3' tails, or assay-specific artifacts that should be removed before alignment or quantification. If the reads are structurally clean and quality remains acceptable across most cycles, documenting the QC results may be more appropriate than trimming. For **long-read data**, the decision is often more selective. You may filter reads by length or quality before assembly, remove adapters or barcodes from Nanopore or PacBio data, or leave the reads unchanged if the quality profile already matches the needs of the downstream workflow. To work through the logic in more detail, start with [Short-read QC and trimming](./short-read/) or [Long-read QC and trimming](./long-read/), then continue to the tool-specific tutorial that matches your data and QC pattern.
